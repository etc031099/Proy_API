## Root cause and behavior

After removing recognized SKU codes and product package sizes, J.2I extracted every
remaining digit token from the prose and required each to match a cited numeric
fact (exactly, or with the approved 1–2 decimal rounding). Therefore list indices
such as `1.` and `2)` were treated like stock or quantity claims. The text safety
check also normalized newlines to spaces, preventing a valid multiline list from
reaching numeric validation unchanged.

The extractor now removes only a list marker at the beginning of a line: `1.`, `2.`,
`3.`, `1)`, `2)`, `3)`, optionally after a Markdown bullet (`- 1.`, `* 2)`, `+ 3.`)
and up to three leading spaces. It strips that marker only; body text and digits on
the same line remain intact. Newlines are allowed between individually sanitized
lines. Other controls remain rejected.

The same classification runs on `interpretation` and `advisoryRecommendation`.
Written ordinals (“primero”, “segundo”, “tercero”) contain no digit tokens and need
no special numeric exemption. A phrase such as “opción 1” is not stripped as a
list marker and is validated as a number against cited facts.

## Quantities remain grounded

“1. Priorizar Cocinero; reponer 40 unidades” ignores only the leading `1.` and
still rejects `40`. “Reponer 1 unidad” and “reponer 2 unidades” remain numeric
claims and fail unless those values occur in the observation's cited evidence.
The same applies to unsupported percentages, risks, stock and forecast numbers.
There is no whitelist for the values 1 or 2. J.2I product-presentation handling
(`900 ml`, `750 g`), fact references, decimal locale and existing rounding policy
remain in place; `5.03 → 5` is still rejected.
